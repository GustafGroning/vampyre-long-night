Vampire the Long Night 


What is this? 
Vampire the long night is a matrix mega roleplaying game for 20 players (and one or more game master) set in the world of darkness. The game can be played over any online forum where text chats can be set up, but for this game Discord is recommended. In the game, each player will create a vampire that is a member of the elite of vampiric society and scheme against other players to grow their own power, influence and resources in order to become the winner of the campaign. 

The game master (GM) is in charge of preparing, facilitating and running the campaign. Their job is to make the game run smoothly and to resolve player actions. 

How does it work? 
Every turn the players decide what actions their character makes and in what order they perform these actions in. Once all players are done, or at a pre-set deadline, all players send in their actions to the GM. The GM resolves all actions and determines what happens as the actions are performed. When the GM is done, they send out an open news update that describes the larger happenings in the game. Every player also gets a personal update related to the information their character and minions get their hands on. With this personal update, players also get the resources, VP and minions that they might have acquired thanks to their actions. This marks the end of the round. Then repeat this process for 8 rounds. The player with the most VP after 8 turns is considered the winner. 

What can you expect from the game?
This is a game about intrigue and manipulation. You will need to maneuver the ever evolving political landscape of kindred society. You will be a powerful vampire but so will every other player. To have a successful campaign you will need to communicate with players, organise plans and share information. Don't go into this game expecting that you will be able to do everything, because you won´t. Use your allies and utilize their abilities for your gain. But beware, backstabbing, lies and cruelty is also a big part of the game. In this game you are alone, only one player can win a campaign and some are willing to do everything to do so.

What is expected from you?
As a player in a Vampire the long night campaign there are a few things expected of you as a player. They are the following:

Don't hold grudges, some players will betray you, it's all a part of the game and fun. Plan your revenge and strike back, but there is no reason to insult anyone personally for their actions in the game.
Answer when someone contacts you in the game. Your answer can be that you are busy and can't give a proper reply. Just don't ghost players when they try to engage with you.
Don't spam the GM or other players. Everyone can ask for a break and you must respect that. 
Send in your actions on time and double check that they are correct and follow the rules. 
Read the rules at least once. 

Is winning important? 
It depends on how you see it. The rules and objectives of the game are there to cause tension and drama in the campaign. The winner gets some hype and their vampire will start in a powerful position in the next game. Play to win but don't let it stop you from having fun!

Actions: 

Actions are the centerpiece of the game. Every turn your vampire performs and minions perform actions. There are 5 different types of actions.

Faction actions: Actions that affect the map, cities, and political landscape of the game. All sect or title specific actions are faction actions.
Personal actions: Actions that your vampyre personally performs.
Minion actions: Actions performed by minions.
Discipline actions: Actions that you need different disciplines to perform. 
Artifact actions: Actions you need different artifacts to perform. 

You can divide your actions in any way you want (just remember that only minions can perform minion actions). 

Pick the actions you want to perform and make sure you can pay for them. (Remember that income comes at the start of the next round) And write all your actions in the order you want to perform them. Actions will resolve from top to bottom. So all players' action nr 1 will happen first, then nr 2 and so on. If something happens so an action cannot be resolved, like a combat that sends you to torpor or you try to target something that's in the wrong city, the GM informs you that the action did not resolve and you get the cost for the action refunded. Make sure you plan out your actions, buffs and modifiers need to be applied before the action they target.

Example of how well written actions look: 
1. Obfuscate: Give +4 stealth to action nr 3. Cost: 0, (Undetectable)
2. King kong (Lvl 1) - Move - New York to Philadelphia - Cost 5 - Stealth +1
3. Move: Move from New York to Washington DC. Cost: 10, Stealth +2
4. Bleed: Bleed Washington DC, Cost 0, Stealth +4 (from Obfuscate)
5. Move: Move from Washington DC to New York, Cost 10 Stealth +2
6. John Smith (Lvl 2) - Bleed - Philadelphia - cost 0 - Stealth + 3

Disciplines and Discipline actions:
Disciplines in Vampire: The Long Night are supernatural powers granted by a vampire’s bloodline and clan. They reflect a vampire’s inhuman nature, ranging from enhanced physical abilities like strength and speed to mystical arts like mind control, shapeshifting, or summoning shadows. 

There are 14 different disciplines in the game. What disciplines you have access to depends on what clan you have chosen and how you build your vampire. When you have a discipline you can spend an action to perform that discipline's specific action. Discipline actions work like other actions. 

Mastered Disciplines: 
A vampyre can spend 1 EXP to master a discipline they already have. It will give them a new mastered action that they can use. A player can only use the (M) discipline action if they have mastered the discipline. 

Stealth and intercept: 
All actions in the game have a stealth value. The total stealth value of any action is the actions stealth value added to the vampire or minion performing the actions value. That's the total stealth of the action, all players in the same city as the acting vampire with an intercept higher or equal to the total stealth learns of the action. 
All cities also have intercept. The controller of a city learns all actions that target and originate in the city that have lower or equal stealth to the city's intercept. 

Trifle:
Some actions have Trifle. The first action you make with Trifle each round doesn't cost an action. 
























COMBAT
It will happen that vampires will have to fight for supremacy! 

There are 6 different steps of combat.  
The stand off 
The Charge 
Combat 
The negotiation 
Press! 
Combat ends. 

The Stand off 
When Vampires engage in combat there is first a dramatic stare down between the fighters! 
The GM will create a combat chat for both players participating in the combat. Informing them that the combat will begin. The players now have up to 6 hours to make a deal before the combat. A deal can be anything. All promised blood, artifacts, minions and cities that are promised will be transferred directly. But any promise of future actions and payments are not binding. 

If the players make a successful deal the combat ends. If not, proceed to the next step. 

The Charge 
The storm is here! 
Both players have 6 hours to spend blood to increase their strength. The players must send a DM to the GM determining how much blood they spend on the combat. For every 10 blood spent they will gain +1 Strength in the combat. Strength gained this way does not follow the rule of 3.

Combat
A clash of claws and fangs! 
In combat the GM rolls one D4 for every side in the combat, then adds the strength of all sides to their D4. The side with the highest result wins the combat. 

The negotiation 
Time for winners to brag and losers to bargain.
The GM gives a description of how the combat played out. Players now have 6 hours to make a deal. A deal can be anything. All promised blood, artifacts, minions and cities that are promised will be transferred directly. But any promise of future actions and payments are not binding. 

If a deal is made the players move to combat ends. If no deal is made, move to the next step. 



Press! 
I didn't hear no bell!
The loser of the combat can decide to press. A press costs 50 blood. repeat steps from step 2 in a new round of combat. If the loser does not press, move to the next step. 

Combat ends
And so it comes to an end
If the players have successfully made a deal the GM transfers all promised blood, artifacts, minions and cities and then the combat is over. 

If no deal was made the losing player rolls on the wounds table. And then combat is over. 

THE WOUND TABEL (Rolled with 2D6)
The GM rolls 2D6 when rolling on the wounds table! 
2-3: The vampire is burned.
4-6: The vampire is sent to torpor in the city the fight took place in or any adjacent city. (their choice)
7-9: The vampire is sent to torpor in their domain or any adjacent city to the city the combat was resolved in (their choice). 
10-12 The vampire flees back to their domain. (If there are multiple options you choose)

Minions in combat: 
Minions vs Vampires
If a minion is in combat it works exactly the same as a vampyre. A minion's strength, stealth and intercept is equal to its lvl for the purpose of hunts and combat. But if a minion is defeated in combat it's burned. Minions can´t spend blood to gain more strength during the charge step or press. A minion whit the meek trait can´t win a combat against a vampier.

Minion VS minion:
All steps except the combat stepped and combat ends step are skipped. In simple terms the GM rolls and resolves the combat, there is no negotiation between servants.  

Minions helping in combat: 
Some actions like, bodyguard. Let's minions fight with their vampire masters. Then the minion(s) adds their lvl to the vampire's strength. If you lose a combat that you have minions in they are all burned. Unless you can save them when pleading for your life. 
TORPOR
A vampire in torpor can´t make actions unless it's the awakened action. When in torpor a vampire can only make one action a turn. But they can still make minion actions.
When a vampire is in torpor it counts as they have 0 VP for the purpose of end game scoring. A vampire can't score VP while in torpor. 

When a vampire tries to awaken from torpor they must do so three times to leave torpor. 
MINIONS
All vampires have servants. 
In the game they are your minions. A minion is a NPC character that serves a player. A minion can be anything from a human in a position of power to a deformed war ghoul serving the vampiric master that created it. 

There are 5 types of minions in the game. GHOULS, HARPIES and EYES are the three generic minion types. PRIMOGENS are minions that only princes can recruit and ARCHONS are minions that only Justicars can recruit. A minion's type determines what actions they can perform and how much upkeep they cost. 

Minions have different levels from 1-3. A level 1 minion represents the weakest servants a vampire can have. Like a group of humans or a newborn vampire. A level 3 minion is the strongest servant a vampire can have, like a war ghoul, an older vampire or the mayor of a city. But all minions gain 1 Minion action each turn! 

Minions don't have stats like players do. A minions lvl represents their stats. So a lvl 2 minion has a strength of 2, stealth 2 and intercept 2. Other modifiers can change this. Every round you must pay an upkeep cost for every minion that you have. 

Minions cost upkeep that must be paid every turn. If upkeep can't be paid the minion is burned. You don't need to pay for a minions upkeep the round you gain it 

Every minion gets one minion action every round and they can ONLY make minion actions that match their minion type.. 

The different minions have different traits.

A vampire can carry any number of artifacts but a minion can only carry one.

THE CITIES
Cities are the different arenas that the camarilla, anarchs and Sabbat will fight over. 
A city has four different stats, Stability, Blood flow, Masquerade status and Intercept. 

City example:
Name: NYC (New york city) 
Controller: ______ Prince of NYC (Camarilla) 
Stability: 4 / 5 
Blood flow: 130 Blood (150 - 20)
Masquerade status: 3/5
Intercept: 1
Problems: Sabbat presens, Anarch presens, Independent Gangrels in central park, To many vampires (-20 Blood flow, -1 MS etch turn). 
Special: Capital of the world ( +1 stealth on all personal actions)

Stability represents how much control the leading vampyre of a city has. To create revolts anarchs will need to lower the stability of the city they want to do it in. If a city ever gets 0 stability it will get a problem. 

Blood flow is how much blood the city's controller gets at the start of each round. 

Masquerade status represents the vampire's control over humans. If a city gets a MS of 0 it triggers a masquerade violation! (see keywords)

Intercept, the city's controller will learn all actions taken against or in the city that have lower stealth than the city's intercept. 

The Controller is the vampyre ruling the city. 

Problems are unique negative modifiers to a city. A city can have any number of problems. 

Specials are modifiers or special effects a city or city's controller gains. A city can have any number of specials. 

Contesting a city:
During the game there might be two or more vampires who claim control of the same city. When a city is contested it's not uncontrolled but no one gets the blood flow from the city. 
A contested city will get -1 stability and -1 MS etch turn its contested. 

There are three ways that someone contesting a city can gain control over it. 
One is to make a deal, a vampire can stop contesting a city at any time for any reason so send them some blood, a minion or maybe some promises and they might leave the city to you. 
Or you can make the battle for control action. 
Or if any vampire contesting the city enters torpor or is burned. A vampire in torpor can´t contest anything.

 If a masquerade violation happens in a contested city all contesting vampires will be regarded as controllers.

Taking a city:
There will come times when a vampyre tries to take another city for themselves. A city is taken only when the controller of the city is changed after being contested. When a city is taken the new controller scores VP. If it's a baron they score 6 VP. If it's a Prince they score 8 VP but any camarilla member can claim credit the next round to steal VP from them. Archbishops score 3 VP for everyone in their sect when they take a city.
YOU CAN ONLY SCORE VP FROM CONQUERING A CITY ONCE PER CITY.

Losing a city: 
If you lose control of a city you will gain the city's full blood flow at the start of the next round. You will lose your title but remain in your sect if it´s your only city.

THE INFORMATION RULE
You are not allowed to send screenshots of conversations, stats or other hidden information. Only open information is allowed to be screenshot and sent. 

You are also not allowed to copy-paste conversations and send them to other players or to read them out allowed. You must always retell them with your own words, but to quote some parts is okay.

THE POWER OF 3 RULE
In the game you vampire can gain a lot of buffs from different actions, artifacts and other modifiers. But a vampire can NEVER gain more than +3 in any stat from any single source. 

A source can be any game effect (actions, artifacts, modifiers, upgrades). For example, a vampire with protean can use it 3 times for +3 Strength, after that the source of protean can´t give the vampire more strength, but you can use an artifact like the black gloves to gain +2 strength for a grand total of +5 strength. As you gain bonuses from 2 different sources. 

DOMAIN: 
All vampyre players will have a domain. Some rules refer to a vampire being in or moving to their domain. Princes, Barons and Archbishops treat cities they control as their domains. Justicars and Inner circle members treat all Camarilla cities as their domain. If something tells you to move to your domain and you have more than one option, you choose.


VP AND HOW TO GET THEM 
Victory points (VP) are how you win the game. The player with the most VPs at the end of a campaign is the winner. There are multiple ways to gain VPs depending on what title you have. If you ever change titles you keep all the VPs you have but must follow your new titles ways of gaining new VPs. 

Inner circle:
Members of the inner circle have the easiest ways of gaining VPs. At the start of the game they begin with 3 VPs. And at the start of every second round they gain 3 more. In every Debate in the Ivory Tower both Inner circle members will always have one option that gives them 1VP in addition to its other effects. 

Justicars: 
Justicars will gain missions that can gain them VPs. Additionally they gain 2 VP every time they send a member of the Sabbat to torpor or burn them. They can also gain VP by exposing members of the Sabbat with the Expose the sabbat! action. 

Princes:
The princes gain VPs by taking control of new cities, it gains them 8 VPs. At the start of every turn that they control more than one city, they gain 1VP for every additional city they control. Princes can build influence in cities they control. When a prince builds influence in a city they will gain an influence in that city. If they control the city at the end of the campaign they gain VP equal to the influence of the city. 

Barons:
The barons gain VPs by taking control of new cities; it gives them 6 VPs. At the start of every turn that they control more than one city, they gain 1VP for every additional city they control. Princes can build influence in cities they control. A Baron gains 2 VPs when they successfully perform the Diablerie action. The Anarch Revolt action also gives VP.

Archbishops:
The Archbishops gain 1 Vp when they burn a minion controlled by a non-sabat
player. They gain 2 Vp if they trigger a masquerade violation in a non-sabbat city.
An archbishop gains 2 VPs when they successfully perform the Diablerie action. 
All Sabbat players gain 3 VP if an Archbishop captures another city. And if a sabbat player burns a member of the inner circle they score 10 VP. The Shovelhead and Esbath actions can also give VP.
 If a sabbat player is sent to torpor by a Camarilla player, all archbishops lose 2 Vp. 

Final score for a campaign
After the final round of the campaign is finished the GM will go over all players VPs. The player with the most VP is the winner of the campaign. A vampire in torpor or that is burned counts as having 0 VP for the final scoring. 
The Camarilla

The Camarilla is a vast and ancient sect of vampires, devoted to maintaining the Masquerade, the law that hides the Kindred from human society. Created in response to mortal inquisitions, it values secrecy, order, and control. The sect is ruled by a secretive Inner Circle, whose will is enforced by the Justicars, near-mythical figures with the authority to judge and investigate princes. Each Justicar commands a network of agents known as Archons, acting as both spies and enforcers. Local power lies with Princes, who govern individual cities with near-absolute authority. To belong to the Camarilla is to live under constant surveillance, endless politicking, and strict traditions that protect the whole from exposure or collapse. For many, it is safe through structure. For others, it is tyranny masked as order. But even this mighty sect now struggles to hold back the storm of rebellion, betrayal, and apocalyptic dread.

There are three titles in the Camarilla, Inner Circle, Justicar and Prince. They all need to work together in order to maintain the strongest sect in the game. The highest ranked members of the camarilla are the Inner Circle members! They are the most powerful vampires when the game starts and can call upon endless resources. Their job is to maintain the camarilla as the strongest sect and to ensure its members remain loyal! They can give rewards and hand out punishments. A member of the inner circle has 6 actions every round.

Justicars are the enforcers of the camarilla! It's their job to hunt down the sabbat and to keep the anarchs at bay! They are given missions that they must accomplish to score VP and gain blood. It's also the responsibility of the Justicars to keep the princes in line and stop them from betraying the sect. They have access to the powerful Arcon minion type to help them stalk enemies to the camarilla and root out sabbat influence.  A Justicar has 4 actions every round.

The Princes are the backbone of the Camarilla. They are the rulers of the sect's many cities and enforce the sect's presence in any one region. They build on their cities to make them grow more powerful and secure. It's also the princess' responsibility to expand the camarilla to cities that are not under camarilla influence to grow the sects and their own power. They can also recruit the Primogen minion to help them build their cities. A prince has 4 actions every round.


The Ivory Tower
The Camarillas special mechanic is the Ivory Tower! The sect uses the Ivory tower in order to push their decrees on all other kindred and rule from the shadows. But the tower is a place for intrigue, backstabbing and plots within plots.

Every round the Ivory Tower will present a Debate. Every debate has 2-4 different options to vote on. When a camarilla vampire writes their actions they must also declare how they vote. A vampire can divide their votes between multiple options. 

A camarilla vampire has votes equal to their title. They can also use the Buy votes action to gain more votes. Non-camarilla vampires can vote in the Ivory tower thanks to this action. When a debate has been resolved it will be revealed how many votes each option got but not who voted for what.

If a member of the inner circle or Justicar would lose their title or be burned a special debate will begin where players can vote on which member of the sect who will take the title. 

No title - 1 Vote
Prince - 2 Votes
Justicar - 3 Votes 
Inner circle - 4 Votes

The Anarchs

The Anarch Movement is a loose, rebellious coalition of Kindred who reject the rigid hierarchy and elitism of the Camarilla. Formed from frustration with centuries of elder domination, the Anarchs fight for freedom, equality, and self-determination among vampires. They despise the Traditions being used as tools of control and see the Princes and Inner Circle as relics of a dying order. While not as violent or chaotic as the Sabbat, Anarch domains often operate with open debate and rotating leadership, prioritizing the voice of the younger generations. However, their lack of unity makes them vulnerable to internal power struggles and outside manipulation. Despite this, the Anarchs continue to grow in strength, especially in cities where the Camarilla has failed to protect or serve the Kindred population. For many neonates, the Movement represents the future of vampire society. One where all Kindred have a say in their fate.

The anarchs only have one title, Baron. The barons want to do one thing… REVOLT! The anarch revolt action is one of the most powerful actions in the game! It can completely destroy another player's turn or city! And the more anarchs that revolt the stronger it gets! So an anarch's game plan should always be to lower the stability of other cities and trigger revolts in them. But the anarchs can be much more than just rebels. Organizing the younger generations of vampires can form powerful armies and using them the anarch cities can become havens for kindred of all kinds. A Baron has 5 actions every round.

It is important to keep in mind that the anarchs are not an organized sect like the camarilla. All Barons need to be able to stand on their own. Temporary alliances can be formed when the Camarilla or Sabbat attack their domains. But these alliances don't last for long. A Baron can trust no one, for one wrong move can mean the end of their domain forever. 

The anarchs don't have a unique sect mechanic like the Camarilla or Sabbat. But to compensate for this they have very strong faction actions that can let them move faster than vampires of the other sects. Speed and specialized strikes are the anarchs strongest weapon against their enemies. 











THE SABBAT 

The Sabbat is a fanatical and violent sect of vampires that rejects the Camarilla's traditions and the Masquerade. Embracing their monstrous nature, the Sabbat see mortals as cattle and believe Kindred should rule openly. Bound by blood and loyalty, Sabbat packs operate like militant cults, often engaging in brutal rites like the creation of Shovelheads, fresh vampires thrown into battle. They favor freedom through strength and despise the tyranny of elders, even while following their own brutal hierarchies. The Sabbat thrives in chaos, waging wars against the Camarilla and spreading fear wherever they take hold. Though diminished in modern nights, whispers of Sabbat resurgence persist—especially as Gehenna looms. To most Kindred, they are nightmares in the dark, monsters who embrace the beast within without shame or restraint.

There is only one title in the Sabbat, Archbishop. But at the start of a campaign all archbishops will be hidden among the other two sects. Disguised as either a Prince or Baron. Archbishops control a city just like the Prince or Baron they are pretending to be. They can use all the actions of the title they masquerade as, but they don´t score VP in the same way. An exposed archbishop has 4 actions every round.

Their goal is to build hidden Sabbat bases in cities with their Build the Base action. A sabbat base can have a Lvl from 1-3. There can only be one Sabbat base per city. All Sabbat players share their bases and will know the location of every base. Some Sabbat actions require a base of certain lvls to be performed. Sabbat players score VP mainly from their special actions that require high lvl bases. 

How is a Sabbat member found? 
A sabbat member can be found in one of two ways. They can reveal themselves when calling a crusade or get exposed by a Justicar with the expose sabbat action. When a sabbat member is exposed they lose their disguise and the title that goes with it. They gain the sabbat tag and lose the ability to use the actions of the sect they had infiltrated.

Sabbat schemes 
The Sabbat scheme is a unique mechanic to symbolize the sects ability to constantly create trouble for the other sects. 

The sabbat players get X+1 extra actions where X is the number of sabbat players. These actions must be divided between the sabbat player before the start of any round. All sabbat players must agree on how the actions will be divided. If players can't agree no one gets the action. How the players agree is up to the sabbat players etch round, it must just be clear to the GM who gets the extra actions.

Keywords: 

Ambush: If you attack a vampire and have higher stealth then there intercept the GM rolls one extra dice for you in combat and picks the best one.

Bleed: When an effect says that you bleed a city. That city's blood flow is lowered by the amount you bleed until the end of the round and you gain that amount of blood. 

Blood flow: A city's Blood flow is how much Blood a city generates for its controller each turn.

Blood hunt: A vampyre can be the target of a blood hunt. All Justicars will get a mission to burn them. It will reward all Justicars a lot of VP. If a non-Justicar player sends the blood-hunted vampire to torpor or burns them they gain 150 blood and 2VP.  If a camarilla member triggers a blood hunt they lose their title. 

Burned: If something is burned it's removed from the game. 

Contest: During the game there might be two or more vampires who claim the control of the same city. When a city is contested it's not uncontrolled but no one gets the blood flow from the city. A contested city will get -1 stability and -1 MS etch turn its contested. Contesting a city controlled by another player costs 50 blood per round you contest it. There are three ways that someone contesting a city can gain control over it. The first one is to make a deal, a vampire can stop contesting a city at any time for any reason so send them some blood, a minion or maybe some promises and they might leave the city to you. Or you can make the battle for control action. Or if all other vampires contesting the city enters torpor or is burned. If someone contests a city you control you will always have an opportunity to move back to their city for the start of the next round. If a masquerade violation happens in a contested city all contesting vampires will be regarded as controllers. 

Domain: All vampyre players will have a domain. Some rules refer to a vampire being in or moving to their domain. Princes, Barons and Archbishops treat cities they control as their domains. Justicars and Inner circle members treat all Camarilla cities as their domain. If something tells you to move to your domain and you have more than one option, you choose. 

EXP: Stands for experience points. All vampires begin with a number of EXP depending on their title. A vampire spends EXP on different upgrades (See character creation). A Vampyre can spend a maximum of 12 EXP during a game.     

Hunt: When you are looking for a vampyre or minion to start combat or make any other action against them you (usually) need to make a hunt roll. The GM rolls a D4 and adds your personal intercept and then rolls a D4 and adds the targeted vampire or minions stealth. If the score is higher than the hunted vampires or minions stealth your action can continue. 

Influence: Camarilla Princes can build influence in their cities with the Build my kingdom action. A city's influence can always be seen by players in city notes. At the end of the game all princes score VP equal to the influence in cities they control. If a prince ever loses a city they lose all influence in that city.   

Intercept: All vampires and cities have an intercept score. You will learn all actions that originated from the city you are in that have lower stealth than your intercept. If you control a city you will learn all actions that target the city and all actions that originated in the city with lower stealth than the city's intercept. 

Masquerade violation: If a city gains a masquerade status of 0/5 it will trigger a masquerade violation. The controller of the city will get a blood hunt called on them for failing their duties to honor the first tradition. All adjacent cities will get -1 MS at the start of each round until the masquerade violation has been mended by giving the city that triggered the violation 5/5 MS. If more than half of all cities have a masquerade violation all vampires will be hunted down by humans and the game is over! The first time a masquerade violation triggers in a Camarilla city no blood hunt is called. 

Minion: A minion is a NPC character that serves a player vampire. A minion can be anything from a human in a position of power to a deformed war-ghoul serving its vampiric master. Minions have different levels from 1-3. A level 1 minion represents the weakest servants a vampire can have. Like a group of humans or a newborn vampire. A level 3 minion is the strongest servant a vampire can have, like a war-ghoul, an older vampire or the mayor of a city. Beside a level, a minion has one of five types. Ghoul, Harpie, Eye, Primogin and Arcon. They have a set cost per turn depending on level and minion type.

Missions: Missions are a special mechanic for Justicars. A mission is something a Justicars must complete to gain blood or to score VPs. There are two ways to get missions, if they get a mission from an Inner circle member or if they get it from the Camarilla (with the Orders from the top action).  

Possession: A vampire can be in possession of artifacts. Other vampires don't know what's in your possession unless they use the actions to uncover it. Or you make actions that reveal it.

Royal Privileges: If a blood hunt is called on a prince they instead lose 1 royal privilege. A Prince may spend a royal privilege to gain 1 extra action for a round.

Stealth: When you make an action it will not be obvious to other vampires what that action is. If your stealth is higher than the intercept of other vampires or the cities they are masters over, they will not know of your action. But they might notice the effects of the action. The actions stealth is your (or your minions) stealth and the actions stealth modifier. 

Torpor: A vampire in torpor can´t make actions unless it's the awakened action. When in torpor a vampyre can only make one action a turn. But they can still make minion actions. A vampire can't score VP in Torpor and counts as having 0 VP for the purpose of end game scoring.

VP: Stands for victory points. Get them to win the game.

Trifle: The first action you make with Trifle each round doesn't cost an action.


Round Break down 

Players send in actions.
1a - Players pay for their actions.
1b - Players pay for their minions. 

Start of round effects resolve. 
2a - City effects are resolved. 
2b - Event effects are resolved. 
2c - Other effects are resolved. 

Actions resolve. 
3a - The GM resolves all players action 1 in a random order. Then repeat the process for action 2,3,4 etc until all actions are resolved. Combats are resolved in order depending on when in the action sequence they happened. Remember that players don´t gain any blood income until step 5. 

End or round effects
4a - The fight for control is resolved.
4b - Player end of round effects resolve. Like (M)Dementation, Dominate and others. 
4c - Justicars check if missions are accomplished. 
4e - Check for a masquerade collapse. 

Income.
4a - Players controlling cities gain the cities current blood flow as income. 
4b - Players gain blood from any Bleed they have performed.
4c - Players gain income from other sources. Like Bottomless wealth, Finished missions, or other actions that generate blood.
